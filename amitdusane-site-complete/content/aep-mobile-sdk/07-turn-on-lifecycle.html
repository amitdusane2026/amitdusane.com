This is the shortest step in the guide, and the reason is worth stating plainly. If the app was initialised with MobileCore.initialize, lifecycle tracking is already on. No call to add, no rule to build, no ticket to raise. The work here is confirming that rather than constructing it, and knowing what to do about the older apps where it is not true.
Why?What does lifecycle actually measure? ↗Can it tell you where installs came from? ↗

Two events carry everything: Application Launch when the app comes to the foreground, and Application Close when it leaves. Session length is the gap between them. Installs and upgrades are flags on the first launch after each. Every number a product team asks for in the first month comes out of those two events.[4]

Confirm it, or fix it
1
Ask Does the app call MobileCore.initialize? If yes, lifecycle is on and this step is finished.
2
Ask Does it call registerExtensions instead? Then lifecycle is manual, and the two calls below have to be present.
The manual pair, for older apps only
iOS · Swift
MobileCore.lifecycleStart(additionalContextData: nil)

MobileCore.lifecyclePause()
Android · Kotlin, from the Activity
override fun onResume() {
    super.onResume()
    MobileCore.lifecycleStart(null)
}

override fun onPause() {
    super.onPause()
    MobileCore.lifecyclePause()
}
Where this goes wrong

An app on the manual pair calls lifecycleStart in one Activity and not the others. Sessions then begin only when a person happens to open that screen first. Launch counts come in low. The numbers look plausible enough that nobody questions them for a quarter.

✓ Ready when

Launch and Close events appear when the app is foregrounded and backgrounded. If the app uses the modern initialiser you will have changed nothing to get here, which is the correct outcome rather than a sign something was missed.
