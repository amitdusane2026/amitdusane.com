Data stops arriving on a Thursday. The app team is asked and answers honestly that nothing changed on their side. A ticket is raised anyway, because that is what happens, and it sits in a sprint queue for eleven days. Somebody finally opens Assurance. The events are leaving the phone perfectly well and being rejected at the Edge. A field had been renamed in the schema three weeks earlier. Nobody in that story was careless. The question was aimed at the wrong team in the first hour. Eleven days went into confirming it.

There is one discipline here, and everything else follows from it. Find where the event stopped before deciding whose problem it is. An event can stop in five places. Only two of them belong to the app team, and the other three are yours, which is the reverse of what most people assume when data goes missing.

Walk it in order
Each rung rules out everything below it. Do not skip to the interesting one.
1 Assurance will not connectThe SDK never started, or started against the wrong property. Check the Environment File ID in the build, then that the deep link handler exists.
2 No events at allRead getConsents first. A pending or refused consent produces exactly this, and looks identical to broken tracking code.
3 Client only, nothing at the EdgeThe datastream selected in the tag property, for that environment. Also the queue: a device with no connection holds events rather than losing them.
4 AEP Error ResponseThe payload did not match the schema. Open the error; it names the field.[17] Almost always a renamed or retyped field, or a path the app spelled differently.
5 Accepted, not in the reportThe service on the datastream. A missing Adobe Experience Platform service sends nothing to the dataset while Analytics keeps working perfectly.
Where this goes wrong

The ticket goes to the app team before Assurance has been opened. They investigate honestly, find nothing, and reply that their side is fine, which is true and takes a fortnight to establish. Meanwhile the fault was a checkbox on a datastream. Open Assurance first, every time. Five minutes there decides whether this is a conversation or a sprint.

✓ Ready when

You can say which of the five rungs a problem sits on before anybody is asked to change code, and you can show the evidence for it. That sentence is the whole reason this guide exists: an analytics practitioner who can locate a fault in somebody else's app is worth more than one who can only report that it is broken.
