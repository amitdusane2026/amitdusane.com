A web launch has a moment. The tag publishes, and from that second every visitor is on the new version.

An app release has no such moment. It has a staged rollout that reaches one percent of users on the first day, a store review that may add days in front of that, then a tail of people who will not update for months and a few who never will.

Launch day starts a slow replacement rather than completing one.

The lines cross somewhere around a month, and the old one never quite reaches zero. Any week-on-week comparison during that period is comparing two different implementations rather than two weeks of behaviour.

Two habits make the next quarter readable

Report on app version from the start, so a strange trend can be checked against adoption in one click rather than argued about.

When a number moves, ask whether the population moved before asking whether the users did.

Where this goes wrong

A metric climbs steadily for three weeks after release and is presented as an improvement. It is adoption. The new version reports something the old one never did, so the line rises exactly as fast as people update, and it flattens the moment the rollout completes. Presenting that as a result once is survivable. Building a target on it is not.

✓ Ready when

The production build is live with the production ID, Assurance shows healthy events from it, and a version breakdown exists before anybody asks for one. The implementation is finished. What has started is the longer job of keeping it honest as the app changes around it.
