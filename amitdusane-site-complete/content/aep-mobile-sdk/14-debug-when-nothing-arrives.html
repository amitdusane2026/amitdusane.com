Data stops arriving on a Thursday. The app team is asked, and answers honestly that nothing changed on their side. A ticket is raised anyway and sits in a queue for eleven days.

Somebody finally opens Assurance. The events are leaving the phone perfectly well and being rejected at the Edge, because a field had been renamed in the schema three weeks earlier.

Nobody in that story was careless. The question was aimed at the wrong team in the first hour.

One discipline, and everything follows from it

Find where the event stopped before deciding whose problem it is.

An event can stop in five places. Only two of them can involve the app team at all, and even those usually turn on a value you supplied. The other three are entirely yours, and all of them are checkable in a browser in minutes.

That is the reverse of what most people assume when data goes missing, and the instinct to raise a ticket first is what costs the weeks.

Walk it in order

Each rung removes everything below it. Do not skip to the interesting one.

1Assurance will not connect. The SDK never started, or started against the wrong property. Check the Environment File ID in the build, then that the deep link handler exists.
2Connects, but no events at all. Read getConsents first. A pending or refused consent produces exactly this, and looks identical to broken tracking code.
3Events on the client, nothing at the Edge. The datastream selected in the tag property, for that environment. Also the queue: a phone with no connection holds events rather than losing them.
4An error response at the Edge. The payload did not match the schema. Open the error; it names the field[17]. Almost always a renamed or retyped field, or a path the app spelled differently.
5Accepted, but missing from the report. The service on the datastream. A missing Adobe Experience Platform service sends nothing to the dataset while Analytics keeps working perfectly.
Where this goes wrong

The ticket goes to the app team before Assurance has been opened. They investigate honestly, find nothing, and reply that their side is fine, which is true and takes a fortnight to establish. Meanwhile the fault was a checkbox on a datastream. Open Assurance first, every time. Five minutes there decides whether this is a conversation or a sprint.

✓ Ready when

You can say which of the five rungs a problem sits on before anybody is asked to change code, and you can show the evidence for it. That sentence is the whole reason this guide exists: an analytics practitioner who can locate a fault in somebody else’s app is worth more than one who can only report that it is broken.
