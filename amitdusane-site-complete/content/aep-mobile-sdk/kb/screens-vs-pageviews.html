--- published: 2026-09-06 lastmod: 2026-09-06 title: "A screen is not a page view" seotitle: "Screens Versus Page Views in Mobile Analytics" description: "They look alike in a report and behave differently in the wild, which is why app session counts and web visit counts never quite line up." slug: "screens-vs-pageviews" type: kb weight: 203 params: group: shift grouplabel: "The mental shift" ---

Both are somebody looking at something. The similarity ends at about that point, and the gap is where reconciliation arguments come from.

A web page has a URL, a referrer, and a load that begins and ends. It is destroyed when the visitor leaves it. A screen has none of those guarantees. It can be pushed onto a stack and returned to without reloading, kept alive in the background while a person answers a call, or presented over another screen that is still there underneath.

Session boundaries diverge for the same reason. On the web a visit ends after thirty minutes of inactivity. In an app, backgrounding ends the session, and backgrounding happens whenever somebody switches apps, takes a call, or locks the phone. App sessions are therefore shorter and far more numerous. That is correct behaviour rather than a measurement fault.

So comparing app sessions to web visits directly will mislead whoever does it, every time, and no amount of configuration fixes it. They are counting different things.

For your implementation: name screens for what they are rather than mirroring your site's page names. A screen that maps neatly onto a web page today will stop doing so at the next redesign of either one.

⚑ Architect’s Decision

Never put app sessions and web visits in one trend line without a note saying what each counts. If somebody wants a single number across both, that is a Customer Journey Analytics question rather than a report suite one.
