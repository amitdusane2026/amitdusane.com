Cross-channel analysis is usually described as a platform capability. In practice it is a piece of text agreed between two teams who have never met.

Sending your own identifier alongside the ECID is what lets one person be recognised across an app, a tablet and a website. The mechanism is an identity map: a value, a namespace, and a claim about whether the person proved who they are.

Three parts are yours to decide rather than the developer’s.

NamespaceThe same text in every channel. If the app writes CustomerID and the website writes customerId, both implementations pass their own tests and the profiles never join.
The valueThe identifier the business already uses, not one invented for analytics, so it can be matched against systems outside Adobe.
Auth stateA real claim rather than a formality. Marking everything authenticated when it is not damages every decision built on it later.

Removing the identity on sign-out matters as much as setting it, and it is the half people forget. A shared phone that keeps the previous person’s identifier gives their behaviour to somebody else.

For your implementation: agree the namespace in writing with whoever owns the web implementation before either side builds anything. It is a five-minute conversation that is very expensive to have late.

⚑ Architect’s Decision

One namespace, agreed once, written down, used by every channel without exception. This is the highest-value decision in a cross-channel implementation and it costs nothing to get right at the start.
