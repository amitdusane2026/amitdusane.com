Cross-channel analysis is usually described as a platform capability. In practice it is a string agreed between two teams who have never met.

Sending your own identifier alongside the ECID is what lets one person be recognised across an app, a tablet and a website. The mechanism is an identity map: a value, a namespace, and a claim about whether the person proved who they are.

Three parts need deciding by you rather than by whoever writes the code. The namespace must be the same string every channel sends. If the app writes CustomerID and the website writes customerId, both implementations pass their own tests and the profiles never join. The identifier should be the one the business already uses, not one invented for analytics, so it can be reconciled with systems outside Adobe. And the authenticated state is a real claim rather than a formality: marking everything authenticated when it is not degrades every downstream decision built on it.

Removing the identity on sign-out matters as much as setting it, and it is the half people forget. A shared device that keeps the previous person's identifier attributes their behaviour to somebody else.

For your implementation: agree the namespace in writing with whoever owns the web implementation before either side builds anything. It is a five-minute conversation that is very expensive to have late.

⚑ Architect’s Decision

One namespace, agreed once, written down, and used by every channel without exception. This is the single highest-value decision in a cross-channel implementation and it costs nothing to get right at the start.
