Content Component Rulebook

Every component on this site, what it looks like, when to use it, and when not to. Written for the Adobe Analytics world, designed to be reused unchanged for Customer Journey Analytics, AEP, or any world built after. Verified against world-learning.css, July 2026.

Why any of this exists

These components are not decoration. Most people who arrive from a search do not read. They scan. Their eye skips prose and stops on anything that looks different. Every rule below is a decision about what stops the eye, and why. A component used well is the difference between a reader finding their answer in ten seconds and closing the tab.

The five rules that govern everything

  1. No new CSS classes in content. Ever.A content author never invents a class. If it is not in world-learning.css, it does not exist.

    The exception, and its conditions: the stylesheet may gain a site-level component, added once, wired into the layout, and recorded in this rulebook. To date there is exactly one: .lupdated. The test: is this a component the whole site needs, added deliberately to the layout? Or is it one author reaching for something on one page? The second is always forbidden.
  2. The header states the content, not the category.A header that names its own category is a wasted line. A scanner's eye lands on it and bounces off.
  3. A component is earned, never decorative.Three warning boxes on one page and the reader stops seeing warning boxes. But do not enforce a number. A subject with four real traps gets four warnings. Judge each one on whether it is earning its place, not against a quota.
  4. Absence is a valid answer.A section whose subject has no screen gets no path box. Forcing a component because other sections have one is the error, not the fix.
  5. Never assume a journey the reader may not have taken.Any back-reference must name the concept, link to it immediately, and recap it in a few words. "As we saw earlier" is simply false to someone who arrived from a search, and most readers arrive from a search.
  6. Nothing ships without a real build.The source passing every check proves nothing. A site can be flawless in source and broken in output. Build it, count the pages, crawl the links. This rule exists because a silent build failure once dropped 103 pages while reporting success.

📷 shot-box

Tier 4 · Content · Product screenshots. Added 14 August 2026, first used on M14 §1.

Structureshot-box > shot-title + shot-frame (containing the <img> and a shot-zoom-btn) + shot-note
The image is inertIt carries pointer-events:none and is never wrapped in a link. Only the button opens anything, and it opens an overlay in the same tab. A screenshot that navigates away on a stray tap feels broken.
Bounded, alwaysThe frame caps height at 360px desktop, 260px mobile, so a wide capture sits clearly inset rather than filling the column. Never let a screenshot set its own size.
Every shot earns a noteIf no shot-note can be written, the screenshot is not earned. The note points ("top right, not top left"), states an absence, or names what changed. It never describes the frame, which is the alt attribute's job. The two must not duplicate.
Crop to the featureNot the whole screen. The image area renders at roughly 290px on a phone, so a full-window capture shrinks about five times and its 12px interface labels become unreadable. One full-screen shot per section at most.
Two per section is plentyA screenshot has to beat the diagram that would otherwise occupy that slot. Colour, recognition and physical position are cases text cannot do. Anything a sentence can carry is not a screenshot.
Never any redactionBlur is not redaction and reads as censorship. Control exposure at source: capture from the Adobe training account, and choose a harmless dimension (Day, Browser, Country) so there is nothing to hide. No credit or permission line under the image.
Naming and filesaal_module14_section01_ss1.png, lowercase, numbers zero-padded to two digits. Lives in static/img/. Originals archived unmodified outside the site folder. Always set width, height and loading="lazy".

The last-updated stamp

The lastmod field, and why it lives on the page

Tier 2 · Required on every section page.

WhereFront matter. One line, per page, owned by that page.
Why per pageThe product moves unevenly. Adobe rewrites classification sets and Module 10 needs a new date. Nothing about VISTA changes for three years and Module 8 must keep its old one, honestly. A single site-wide date would be a lie on most pages.
Why not from gitHugo can derive it from the git commit date automatically. Do not. Fix one typo and the date jumps to today, telling every reader "this changed" when nothing did. A date that lies is worse than no date, because it trains people to ignore it. The manual discipline is the point.
Why no version numberA version needs a changelog to mean anything, and a changelog nobody maintains is worse than nothing. The date already answers the reader's real question: can I trust this today?
Front matter
---
slug: "designing-the-key"
title: "Designing the Key"
lastmod: 2026-07-15
description: "..."
---

Last updated: 15 July 2026

Weight12.5px, muted. Visible, never dominant. The date is semibold so the eye finds it; the label is not.
MarkupA real <time datetime="2026-07-15">, so search engines read it as a machine-readable date rather than guessing from prose.
Dark themeNeeds its own override. --text3 (#64748b) is too dim against #0f172a.

The two constraints that took the site down

The lastmod trap that silently deletes your entire site

Read this twice. It dropped 103 pages at once, and the build reported success.

By default, Hugo derives a page's date from lastmod, then derives publishDate from date. So a lastmod that is even one day ahead of the build server's clock gives the page a future publish date, and Hugo silently drops it from the build. No error. No warning. The page simply 404s.

✗ What actually happened
GitHub Actions builds in UTC. The dates were written in IST (UTC+5:30). Any page dated "today" in IST is "tomorrow" in UTC for five and a half hours of every single day. All 103 section pages vanished. The only visible symptom was empty navigation dropdowns.
✓ The fix, in hugo.toml. Not optional. 
[frontmatter]
  date = ["date", "publishDate"]
  lastmod = ["lastmod"]
  publishDate = ["publishDate", "date"]
  expiryDate = ["expiryDate"]
This decouples lastmod from the date cascade permanently. lastmod now means only "when was this content last revised", which is all it should ever have meant. Do not remove these lines.

Nothing ships without a real build

Static inspection of the source is not verification.

The source can pass every check and the site can still be broken, because the bug lives in the interaction between source and build. Every check written against the source passed. The site was still dead.

The gate, in order
1. Buildhugo --gc with the pinned binary. Never --minify.
2. Assert the countIf pages generated is fewer than pages expected, stop. This single check would have caught the outage in one second.
3. Crawl the outputCheck the built HTML for dead links, not the source.
4. Confirm the navigationEvery module, every section, rendered.
5. Confirm aliasesIf any slug changed, the redirect must exist.
Hugo installs in about thirty seconds. There is no excuse for skipping this.

Hard technical constraints

SVG colour must go in a style attribute, with a hex fallback

The single most common rendering failure on this site.

✗ BROKEN. Does not resolve. The element renders black or invisible. 
<rect fill="var(--card)" stroke="var(--accent)"/>
✓ CORRECT 
<rect style="fill:var(--card, #ffffff);stroke:var(--accent, #e11d48);stroke-width:2"/>
<text style="fill:var(--text1, #0f172a);font-size:12px;font-family:sans-serif">Label</text>
Why: a CSS var() does not resolve inside an SVG presentation attribute. It only resolves inside a style attribute. The hex fallback covers the case where the variable is unavailable.
Every SVG also needs
<svg viewBox="0 0 700 250" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;margin:4px 0 8px"
     role="img" aria-label="Plain-language description of what the diagram shows">

Three more that will bite you

Build commandhugo --gc   Never hugo --minify. The minifier destroys inline SVG text elements.
No nestingA code-block inside a warn-box, pro-tip, or info-box is untested and unrendered. State the rule in the box; put the code block after it, at normal level.
Inline overridesPermitted, sparingly, on an existing class. Always carry the hex fallback:
<div class="strategy-header" style="background:var(--accent, #e11d48);color:#fff">
Asset URLs carry a content hashDo not remove the ?v= on the stylesheet and script tags in partials/head.html and _default/baseof.html. Without it the URL never moves when the file changes, so browsers serve a stale copy indefinitely and a CSS edit appears to do nothing. On 14 Aug 2026 that made a brand new component look completely broken while the built output was correct the whole time, and it would have left every returning visitor on the old stylesheet in production. Verify styling on the delivered page, never on a page you have patched by injecting CSS — that proves the CSS works when applied, not that the page applies it.
Content files are .htmlNever write component markup into a .md file. hugo.toml has no [markup.goldmark.renderer] block, so unsafe defaults to false and Goldmark silently deletes every raw HTML element, leaving <!-- raw HTML omitted --> in the output. No error, no warning. This shipped live on all five category landing pages, which lost both intro paragraphs each. Fixed August 2026 by renaming them to _index.html. Module index files may stay .md only while their bodies contain no markup.

Voice and prose rules

These travel with the components. A component with bad copy in it is worse than no component.

No dashesNo em-dashes or en-dashes anywhere. Not in prose, not in tables, not in SVG. Use commas, colons, or a full stop.
No author voiceNo I, my, we, our, us. Reader-facing you only.
Exception: quoted speech. A team saying "we set up a CNAME" is a character speaking, and stays.
American spellingThroughout.
Link casingA link whose anchor names a module or section uses that section's exact Title Case. A genuine common noun ("inside report suites") stays lowercase.
LengthA requirement of the topic, never apologized for in prose. A short section is correct when the subject is short.
AnalogiesThey arrive, or they do not. Never placed into a designated slot.

Porting to a new World

  1. Copy world-learning.css unchanged.Then change one line: --accent. That is the World's identity. Crimson for Analytics. Blue for the Migration Guide. Something else for CJA.
  2. Do not change the semantic colours.--warning, --info, --success, and the path-box violet stay fixed. A reader who learns "amber means this will hurt you" in one World must find it true in the next, or they relearn your visual language every time.
  3. Every rule above carries over unchanged.The components are product-agnostic. Only the accent and the content change.

18 August 2026 (2)The byline, and the end of hand-copied chrome. Every section and every landing now carries an author line, which closes the site's largest credibility gap: a search lander previously read three thousand words of technical judgment with no indication of who wrote it. Sections get name, credential, publish date and read time; landings get "Created by" and the credential only, because a signpost has no publish history worth stating. The name carries by size, a step above the metadata around it, not by being coloured and underlined like a link, which made the byline read as a row of links. Read time is computed once at build time and published on data-readmins; counting words a second time in the browser produced 2,929 against 3,001 and showed the reader 13 minutes in one place and 14 in another, which is exactly the kind of small contradiction that costs a page its authority. Dates are short form, and the Updated date appears only when it differs from Published, so launch day does not render the same date twice on 116 pages. Added TechArticle structured data with datePublished and dateModified; a section previously emitted BreadcrumbList and nothing else. The monogram moved into a single partial after living in six copies, and the About control now carries the mark alone with no disc, at double the previous size. The floating controls have an explicit order of loudness — pocket map, then page nav, then identity — and a utility for moving inside one page must never outweigh the site's identity. Standardised the name on Amit G Dusane, no dot, in eleven places that disagreed.
18 August 2026The v2 reading design, rolled across the learning world. Added the type and spacing token tables: nothing in a component may hard-code a px any more. Body prose inside a box is now body-sized rather than 15px, because a warn-box is often the most important thing on the page. One column width for prose and figures alike, at 760px; two attempts to give prose its own narrower measure were rejected as looking broken, and the honest consequence is a measure of about 94 characters rather than the ideal 65–75, traded deliberately to keep the infographics full size. Adopted IBM Plex Sans and Mono, self-hosted, because a system stack renders as a different typeface on every operating system and so cannot carry an identity. Added the in-page spine, which is generated from the h3 stack and never authored, and with it two new obligations on heading wording: the first clause before a comma must stand alone, because the spine cuts there to build its label, and heading text is slugified into a permanent anchor at build time, so rewording a published heading breaks links to it exactly as changing a slug does. The path-box is now linked from the spine as its last entry, so its path-title should read plainly. Recorded a new hard constraint after it took the mobile layout down: a grid item with a pixel max-width does not shrink below its track, and needs max-width:min(…,100%).
14 August 2026Added the shot-box component for product screenshots, the first images on the site, together with its rules: the image is inert and never linked, the frame caps height so a capture cannot set its own size, every shot earns a note that points rather than describes, crop to the feature because the image area is about 290px on a phone, two per section is plenty, and exposure is controlled at capture rather than by blurring. Added the hard constraint that asset URLs must carry a content hash, after a stale cached stylesheet made a correct build look broken, and the checklist item that follows from it: verify on the delivered page, never on one patched by injection. Corrected the checklist count, which said eighteen and listed twenty.
