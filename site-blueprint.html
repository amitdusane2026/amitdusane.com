Purpose

What the site is for, and who reads it

The reader is one specific person: somebody who has already been through Adobe's documentation, still has questions, and finds the topic rigid and boring. Not a beginner. Not somebody who needs convincing Adobe Analytics exists. Somebody stuck.

Adobe writes reference material for people who already know what they need. That is a permanent constraint rather than a failing, and it is exactly why the gap here is durable. The site's job is to make that documentation legible, then hand the reader back to it equipped. Most readers arrive from a search engine on one page, having read nothing else.

Editorial rules that shape the visual design

  • Second person only, zero first person. 1,756 instances of "you" and zero of "I" across the learning world.
  • Components are load-bearing, never decorative. A warning, an analogy, a diagram or a tip is there to build a mental model that survives the reader closing the tab. Each one has to be earned.
  • Opinions, not options. The site takes a position and says which thing to use.
  • No H1 or H2 inside body content. The layout supplies both. A section is a flat stack of h3.subsec-title blocks, typically 5 to 17 of them.
  • Landing pages are directional only, one or two paragraphs, no theory. Knowledge lives in sections.
  • No em-dashes or en-dashes anywhere. Verified at zero occurrences across the whole corpus.
Architecture

Two worlds, one shell

A "world" is a self-contained sub-site selected by URL prefix, with its own stylesheet, JavaScript, page shell and print document. The switch is an if/else chain on hasPrefix .RelPermalink in layouts/partials/head.html and layouts/_default/baseof.html. A reader inside one world never sees the other's chrome.

The porting rule matters for any new design work: a third world copies world-learning.css and changes --accent only. The warning, info, success and path colours stay fixed across every world, so a reader never has to relearn the visual language.

Section page anatomy, in order

  • Untitled opener — one to three paragraphs before the first heading. Never a definition. Either a scenario, an extended metaphor, or a failing report shown before the feature is named.
  • The h3 stack — 5 to 17 h3.subsec-title blocks, each 2 to 6 paragraphs, interleaved with earned components. Heading wording is editorial, often a question or a promise, never a label.
  • The practical walkthrough — second to last heading. A numbered, ordered code-block answering "what do I actually do". Treated as the engine of the section, not an appendix.
  • Synthesis and onward link — final heading, named by function rather than topic. Its last paragraph names and links a related section.
  • path-box, then ref-box — always last, exactly one ref-box, nothing after it. The ref-box holds 2 to 4 official Adobe links and triggers an auto-injected doc-note above itself.

Hard constraints. Never nest a code-block inside a box. SVG colour must live in a style attribute with a hex fallback, because fill="var(--accent)" as a presentation attribute does not resolve reliably. Every SVG carries role="img" and a full-sentence aria-label. Screenshots are never wrapped in a link and never redacted; the image is inert and only a zoom button opens the overlay.

Constraints

Non-negotiables for anyone touching this build

  • Hugo v0.123.7 extended, no theme, no shortcodes. New content files are .html, never .md: hugo.toml has no Goldmark renderer block, so raw HTML is silently stripped from Markdown.
  • Never build with --minify. The minifier destroys inline SVG text elements.
  • description in front matter is the publish flag. A page without one renders dimmed and unclickable in both maps. It is why the 32 unwritten sections are invisible rather than broken.
  • A new module must be added to [params.phases] in hugo.toml. Miss it and the module appears in navigation but vanishes from both maps, with no build error.
  • Asset tags carry a ?v= content hash. Remove it and every returning visitor keeps the old stylesheet after any CSS change.
  • The [frontmatter] block in hugo.toml is load-bearing. Without it a lastmod one day ahead of the UTC build clock silently drops the page. This once removed 103 pages while the build reported success.
  • Push to main publishes immediately. There is no staging environment. develop publishes nothing, which is why the learning world can sit unfinished in the open.
