Design and SEO Decisions

This is a closed record, not a work plan. It began as the design review of 17 August 2026 and grew to 106 items. On 21 August Amit closed it and discarded the 55 that were still open or partial. What survives is the 51 that were decided, shipped, or deliberately rejected — kept because they explain why the site looks the way it does.

Do not treat anything here as outstanding work, and do not propose these items back. The remaining ideas are Amit’s to raise once the content is finished. The full original list is in git history if one is ever wanted by name.

Closed at commit 89b98b1 on develop · 219 pages · 84 of 116 sections written · learning world unpublished. Counts are computed from this document, never typed by hand.

Why this stopped

A list that can only grow will always argue itself into more work. Every review mechanism on this site adds and none subtracts, so 106 defensible items turned into a debate about whether its own items were needed, while the actual job — writing the remaining 32 sections — stood still. The design work that shipped was real and is recorded below. The planning around it had stopped paying for itself.

TYP

Typography and reading

6 kept

The measured core of the problem. These four P0 items are the highest-impact work on the entire list, they introduce no new components, and they are reversible. Two independent design reads arrived at the same three fixes.

TYP-01Introduce a real type scale P0SEOMdone 17 Aug
Why
Measured: h1 is 22px and everything else on the page is 16px. subsec-title 16/600, warn-hdr 16/700, diagram-title 16/700, ref-title 16/700, body 16/400. A section heading is the same size as the sentence under it, so on a 13-screen page the eye has no landmarks. This is the largest single cause of the reading fatigue we are trying to remove.
What
Define a token-based scale with roughly a 1.4–1.5 ratio. Page title, section heading, body, small, and a distinct label size. Every component takes its size from a token rather than a hardcoded pixel value.
Scope
All 116 sections + every page in the learning world
SEO
Heading prominence is a readability and content-structure signal, and it is what makes a page scannable for the search lander who is the largest audience segment.
Result
Eight tokens on :root, with a mobile step and a print step. Desktop: title 30px, heading 21px, body 16px, supporting 14px, label 13px, meta 12px, micro 11px, code 13px. Ratios 1.43 and 1.31 at the top, where they matter. Mobile redefines the same eight tokens rather than eight components, and body prose rises from 13px to 15px — 13px was too small for long-form reading on the rendering Google indexes. Print gains its own point-based step, without which it would have inherited the 30px screen title.

It also uncovered a live cascade fault. body.world-learning .lcontent p and … li are specificity (0,2,2) and were silently beating every component rule at (0,1,1). So .lcrumb asked for 13px and rendered at 16px, .ref-box li asked for 13px and rendered at 16px, and the three box-prose rules asking for 15px were dead declarations. Six rules re-scoped so their tokens actually reach the page.

Verified on the delivered page at 1280px and 375px, in both themes, across a section, a module landing, and a page with body lists. Build 219 pages, no horizontal overflow, no data-newblock. Not included: the ~40 bespoke diagram internals, which stay hardcoded and belong to CMP-01 and CMP-05, and the injected doc-note, which uses !important.
TYP-03Give headings rhythm and rest stops P0Sdone 18 Aug
Why
Measured: subsec-title has 16px above and 10px below, which is tighter than the gap between two paragraphs in places. Headings do not own any whitespace, so they do not function as rest stops.
What
Substantially more space above each heading, and consider a hairline rule above it so each subsection reads as a discrete unit. Spacing comes from a token scale, not per-element margins.
Scope
All 116 sections · 588 headings
TYP-04Adopt a self-hosted typeface: IBM Plex Sans and IBM Plex Mono P1SEOMdone 18 Aug
Why
The site currently uses a system stack, so it renders as Segoe UI on Windows, SF on Mac and Roboto on Android. A system stack cannot carry an identity, which is the requirement that matters most given this design has to serve future sites too. Plex was drawn for technical documentation, has character without being fashionable, its mono sibling shares the same skeleton, and at eight years old it already shows it will not date. It is also clearly not Adobe's typeface, which matters for the no-implied-endorsement position.
What
Subset woff2, self-hosted in static/, two or three weights maximum. No CDN, consistent with the site never loading an external asset.
Scope
Entire site, both worlds
SEO
Must not regress Core Web Vitals. Pairs with PERF-02, which is the guard.
TYP-06Make mono the label voice P1Mdone 18 Aug
Why
Inherited from the external draft and the strongest cheap idea in it. Setting every eyebrow, box header, table header, figure caption and path label in mono uppercase with letter-spacing is what makes a page read as an engineering field manual rather than a blog. It costs nothing and it is the most efficient identity move available.
What
Apply to warn-hdr, info-hdr, pro-tip-hdr, path-title, ref-title, diagram-title, code-lang, shot-title, table headers and breadcrumb.
Scope
10 components × 116 sections
Result
Built twice. Measured first: warn-hdr, info-hdr, pro-tip-hdr, ref-title, path-title, diagram-title and shot-title were all 13.5px / 700 / Plex Sans with letter-spacing normal. Seven components, one typographic object, distinguished only by a colour — and four of those colours were below AA.

The first version followed this item literally and was rejected. Mono, uppercase, tracked, at weight 400. Amit's call on 19 Aug, and it is the more important half of this entry: mono with capitals and letter-spacing is a recognisable generated-page signature. It appears constantly in LLM-produced HTML, a reader who has seen a few will place it, and a site whose whole claim is sixteen years of practice cannot afford a typographic tell that says otherwise. Uppercase went with it, being the same tell in another form. The idea came from the external draft and read well in the abstract; it did not survive contact with what the site is for.

What shipped instead: the site's own face, reading as a label through weight and size rather than case or tracking, which is how print technical publishing has always done it. Plex Sans 700, sentence case, --fs-label stepped 13.5px → 15px (mobile 12.5 → 13.5) so it separates cleanly from 18px body prose. --fs-label has exactly seven consumers, all of them these labels, so the step is contained.

The colour came back with it, which matters because the first version had drained every label to grey — including all 124 pro-tip headers, and that was most of why the site had stopped looking crimson. The failing semantics now use their -ink companions rather than going neutral: warn-hdr 1.91 → 5.42:1, info-hdr 3.15 → 5.73, ref-title 2.25 → 5.56. --warning, --info and --success are untouched either way, so COL-03 holds.

Uppercase survives in two places only, where it is a convention rather than a style: table column headers and the code-block language label. Both in sans.
TYP-07Review body size and line-height P1Sdone 18 Aug
Why
Currently 16px at 1.6. For a long-form teaching site, 17px at roughly 1.7 is usually more comfortable, and the external draft independently chose exactly that. Interacts with TYP-02, since raising the size at a fixed pixel width lowers the character count.
What
Test 16 and 17 against a real 3,000-word section in both themes before deciding. Do not decide from a specimen.
Scope
All reading surfaces
TYP-08Font loading strategy P0SEOSdone 18 Aug
Why

What

Colour

5 kept

Verdict already reached: keep crimson, deepen it, and make it work. A crimson site in a category where every tool is blue is genuinely differentiating.

COL-01Deepen the crimson and move it off a framework default P1Sdone 18 Aug
Why
#e11d48 is Tailwind's rose-600 verbatim, which is the same "unconsidered default" tell as reaching for Inter. It also reads a little bright and consumer-facing against the seriousness the site is aiming at.
What
Nudge deeper and slightly less saturated to a custom value. Do not go as far as the external draft's #96222F, which is dark enough to read brownish as a link colour and loses the clickable signal.
Scope
Learning world accent token
Result
#e11d48 → #ba2142, hsl(347,70%,43%). Hue held so this is a deepening, not a rebrand of 84 signed-off sections; saturation 77→70, lightness 50→43.

It was not only a framework default, it was failing. .lmain sets no background, so the reading column paints on --bg1 (#f8fafc) rather than white, and #e11d48 there measures 4.49:1 — under the 4.5 AA line for normal text, on every inline link in the corpus. Now 5.93:1 on the ground and 6.21:1 on a card, and still well short of the rejected #96222F (7.82:1) that reads brownish.

Six rules carried rgba(225,29,72,…) as a literal and would not have followed the token at all; two content files hardcoded the hex. Both swept.

20 August: this item is marked done and the sweep is not finished. Amit's decision is to defer the remainder to launch prep. Traced properly, the leftover #e11d48 is not the migration world's accent — that is blue, #1f5fae. It is the personal brand crimson, and it styles the monogram gradient, the byline, the About nav link and About page, the author quote, the home marks and CTA, and the print document rules. world-shell.css holds 18 of them and head.html's inline :root holds --crimson plus a brandmark gradient. That inline block is the {{ else }} branch of the INF-06 world lookup, so it serves exactly the pages in neither world: the home page, the About page and the 404.

The consequence is that one brand colour currently has two values on the live site. The same monogram, byline and About panel render #ba2142 on the 116 learning sections, via the chrome.css sweep of 20 August, and #e11d48 on the home page, the About page and every migration page. The rose is the value that measured 4.49:1 and failed AA, so this is a live accessibility defect on the front door and on the page EAT-06 wants as the authority hub. hugo.toml's brandColor is the same hex and is INF-08, so the two should be closed together.

Why it is deferred rather than done: finishing it changes the appearance of pages that are already published, which is a launch-prep decision rather than a design-phase cleanup. It is recorded here so it is scheduled, not rediscovered. The generalisable half, and this is the fourth instance in three days: a sweep that changes a value is only as complete as its file list, and nobody records the file list.

COL-06Contrast audit, both themes, after every colour change P1SEOSdone 18 Aug
Why
Body prose currently uses the secondary ink rather than the primary, which is a deliberate softening but leaves less headroom than it looks. Any accent or neutral change can quietly push a component under AA.
What

Identity and branding

3 kept

Branding is currently thin and you were right about it. The answer is not more logo; it is a coordinate system, dignified diagrams, and a consistent label voice.

IDN-02Commit to a module numbering policy before IDN-01 P0—decided 20 Aug
Why
A printed coordinate is a promise of stability, and this curriculum's numbers have moved before: the restructure renumbered Classifications from M10 to M11 and Analysis Workspace from M13 to M14, and inserted M15. If someone cites 06.5 and a later insert shifts it, every citation breaks silently.
What
A decision: new modules are appended at the end of a phase, never inserted. If that is unacceptable, IDN-01 should not proceed — it is better to have no coordinate than an unreliable one.
Scope
Curriculum governance · gates IDN-01
Result
Amit's decision, 20 August 2026: append-only, with aliases as the escape hatch. New modules are appended at the end of their phase rather than inserted, so a printed coordinate is a real promise. If a future restructure genuinely has to renumber — because the curriculum teaches better in a different order — that is allowed, and it is handled by the existing aliases mechanism so no URL ever 404s.

This is deliberately the weaker of the two promises on offer. The stronger one, never renumber under any circumstance, would have frozen curriculum order against pedagogy permanently, and the curriculum is still being written. The cost is that a coordinate is stable in practice rather than guaranteed in principle. IDN-01 is unblocked and may proceed, and IDN-03 with it.

IDN-07Give the About mark an affordance without giving back its container P1Mdone 19 Aug
Why
Raised by Amit, 19 Aug 2026. IDN-04 removed the disc behind the monogram, which was right — the mark is a distinctive enough shape to stand on its own, and the disc only ever existed to hold a generic glyph. But it cost the control its affordance: a bare logo sitting in a corner does not read as a button, and nothing invited the click.
What
A permanent halo rather than a hover-only one, because an affordance that appears on hover does not exist on a phone, where there is no hover at all. Plus a hover gesture that confirms there is something underneath.
Scope
Site-wide · static/chrome.css · all four page types, both themes
Result
A radial-gradient pseudo-element, not a box-shadow: a box-shadow spreads from the button's square box and would bloom as a rounded square around an 88px button, where a gradient gives a true circle and lets the falloff be tuned until there is no visible rim. It carries pointer-events:none, so it is light rather than hit area.

Two rounds, and the first was wrong on both counts Amit named. It was crimson, which fought the mark: the monogram is navy, so a red halo behind a blue letterform reads as a ring someone applied rather than as light coming off the mark. It is now indigo — #6366f1 light, #818cf8 dark — which is not a new colour so much as one the site already had, in the tagline accent and the path violet, and which sits under navy comfortably.

It also looked like a drawn circle, and there was a measurable reason rather than a matter of taste: the gradient died at 78% of its radius while still carrying .18 alpha, and that surviving step was the edge. The falloff now runs in eight stops approximating a gaussian and reaches true zero at the full radius, the halo is wider (132% → 170%) so the same light spreads over more pixels, peak alpha is halved to .20, and a 5px blur removes any banding left at the outer stops. Wide and weak reads as light; small and bright reads as a disc.

Hover: the halo swells once and then breathes at 3.2s — deliberately slower than a heartbeat, fast enough to notice and slow enough never to nag — while the mark lifts 3px and lights. The transform sits on the SVG rather than the button, because scaling the button drags the halo with it and the pair move as one flat sticker instead of a mark rising out of its own light. prefers-reduced-motion keeps the whole affordance and drops only the movement.

A real bug fixed on the way, which is what prompted the item: in dark mode the hover glow had never fired at all. [data-theme="dark"] .author-fab svg and .author-fab:hover svg are the same specificity and the dark rule sat later in the file, so it won every time. Same source-order trap already recorded in CLAUDE.md.

The phone mark went 80px → 60px in the same pass: 80px was a fifth of a 390px viewport for a control nobody came for. 40px was tried first and went too far, reading as a generic icon rather than the site's mark.

The glow stays indigo on every world, including the blue migration world, because this is the personal-brand mark rather than a per-world control. Verified on the delivered page by driving a real pointer, in both themes, at 360/390/560/561/1280: colours resolve, the click still opens the overlay on all four page types, and the halo spills 6–9px past the viewport corner at its peak with zero page overflow, because it lives inside a fixed element.

Note against WAY-07 and EAT-07: this deliberately raises the About mark's presence, which cuts across the recorded order of loudness (pocket map, then page nav, then identity). Amit asked for it and it is his call, but WAY-07 should be decided knowing the identity mark is now louder than it was.

6 kept

The largest audience is the search lander who wants an answer and leaves. Today a section is 13 screens with no in-page map, no anchors and a dead search button. This category is where that reader is served or lost.

WAY-01Build the in-page spine P0SEOMdone 18 Aug
Why
A section is 9,265px, roughly 13 screens, with 9 headings and no way to see or reach them. You were sceptical because Experience League's version is dry and you do not use it — but theirs is dry because their headings are labels. Yours are written as questions and promises by rule, so the same component produces the argument of the section rather than a list of nouns. Your own reading style is expert behaviour; the site is written for someone stuck.
What
Use the empty right side of the desktop viewport. Active and read states as the reader scrolls. Built from the existing h3 stack so it needs no authoring.
Scope
All 116 sections
SEO
Directly serves the dominant search segment, and the anchors it depends on feed Google's jump-to links.
WAY-02Give every heading a stable ID and anchor P0launch blockerSEOSdone 18 Aug
Why
Measured: 0 of 9 headings on a section carry an id. So there is nothing to deep-link, nothing to bookmark for the returning reader, and nothing for Google to build a jump-link from. The cheapest SEO win available and a usability win for the same reason.
What
Slugified, stable IDs, generated from heading text at build time rather than at runtime so they exist in the served HTML. Stability matters: an ID that changes when a heading is reworded breaks any link that was made to it.
Scope
588 headings across 116 sections
SEO
Enables the "jump to" links Google renders under a result, which materially raises click-through on long pages. Must be done before indexation, since anchors that appear later gain nothing retroactively.
WAY-03A mobile answer for the in-page spine P0SEOMdone 18 Aug

The external draft hides its right rail below 1240px, so the feature that serves the largest audience disappears exactly where most of that audience is. Under mobile-first indexing the phone rendering is the indexed page. This is the biggest gap in their solution and we must not inherit it.
What
A collapsed "On this page" placed above the first heading on narrow screens. Same data, different presentation, not a second component.
Scope
All 116 sections, mobile and tablet
SEO
Mobile-first indexing. A desktop-only feature is, for ranking purposes, a feature that does not exist.
WAY-04Add scroll-padding-top for the fixed header P0Sdone 18 Aug

Components

7 kept

The class vocabulary is disciplined and does not need replacing. The work here is elevating what is undersold, and pricing one real maintenance liability.

CMP-01Elevate the diagram component P1Mdone 18 Aug
Why
136 diagram blocks and roughly 40 bespoke layouts are the site's genuine differentiator — nobody else publishing Adobe Analytics content has this. They currently sit in a plain bordered box under a 16px bold title, presented identically to a warning or a tip. The thing that makes the site unlike anything else looks like furniture. This is where the design budget should go.
What
Give the diagram its own visual register: caption treatment, frame, and space. Pairs with IDN-03.
Scope
136 diagram blocks
Result
The diagram stops looking like an alert. It was a 1px card at radius 10 under a 13.5px bold title — the same weight as a warn-box, quieter than a pro-tip.

It is now a plate: tighter radius, --sp-xl of air above and below so it owns space the way a heading does, and a ruled caption in the label voice. The distinction is deliberately structural, not coloured. Every other box on a section is a wash of its semantic colour, so a plain plate with air and a rule is already unlike all of them, it stays unlike them in print and in dark, and it leaves the accent free for COL-02.

Not done: letting the figure bleed wider than the prose column on a large screen. That touches the three-column grid, which is where the 18 Aug mobile-overflow bug came from, so it wants its own pass.
CMP-02Let diagrams scroll rather than squash on mobile P1SEOSdone 18 Aug
Why
A wide SVG shrunk to a 340px phone is unreadable, and the mobile rendering is the indexed one.
What
overflow-x:auto on diagram-content, with the container scrolling rather than the page body. Never allow the page itself to scroll sideways.
Scope
136 diagram blocks
SEO
Horizontal page overflow is a mobile usability failure Search Console reports directly.
Result
Delivered inside CMP-12 rather than on its own, and at every width rather than only on mobile. .diagram-content, and any .diagram-box holding a direct-child SVG, scroll horizontally with overscroll-behavior-x:contain. The page itself never scrolls sideways, asserted at 360/390/768/1243/1244/1600 across 8 page types.

One detail worth keeping: justify-content:center on a flex container clips an overflowing child so it can never be scrolled to. margin-inline:auto centres when there is room and does not clip when there is not.
CMP-04Re-check shot-box under the new type scale P1SEOSdone 19 Aug
Why
The screenshot component was tuned against the current 16px-everything typography and a 293px mobile image area. Changing the measure and scale changes both. The external draft had no screenshot treatment at all, so nothing can be inherited here.
What
Re-verify frame height caps, the note treatment and the zoom affordance against the new scale, on a real phone width. All 14 existing shots must still read.
Scope
14 screenshots, M14 only, growing
SEO
Confirm alt text quality and explicit width/height at the same time, per SEO-10.
Result
Markup was already strong: all 14 files present as .webp, explicit width and height, loading="lazy", and genuinely descriptive alt text that describes the frame as the rulebook requires. Title and note scale correctly under the new type scale (15px / 13.5px on a phone).

The re-check found a real defect the item did not anticipate: a 334px layout shift on every screenshot, on first visit. Measured cold at 1280 — a lazy image whose used width is auto has no intrinsic size until its bytes arrive, so it occupied 2px and then jumped. The width and height attributes exist precisely to prevent this and could not be used while the width was auto.

Fixing it took two steps, and the second only surfaced by testing. Making width definite lets the attribute-derived aspect ratio reserve the height — but a max-height then clamps the box after the ratio has reserved it, trading one shift for an opposite one. So the height cap became a width cap: --shot-cap multiplied by each image's own aspect ratio, with --sw and --sar set inline per image and derived mechanically from the attributes already in the markup.

Verified cold, with the stylesheet force-refreshed, on a normal image (1500×938), a tall one (628×1294) and a wide one (1400×263), at 1280 and 390: zero shift everywhere, nothing upscales, and the final rendered sizes are identical to what the old max-height produced. This changes what the browser knows in advance, not what the reader sees.
CMP-06Audit every SVG for hardcoded colour P1Mdone 19 Aug
Why
The rulebook requires var(--token, #fallback) in a style attribute, and the last audit found zero violations. Worth re-running because the external draft's very first diagram hardcodes #96222F, #4C555C and #C9D0CC, which would be nearly unreadable in its own dark theme. A capable model produced a design system whose opening figure breaks in dark mode; our constraint caught it. Every token change is a chance to reintroduce that.
What
Re-run after any colour work, and confirm each of the 65 inline SVGs still resolves in both themes on the delivered page.
Scope
65 inline SVGs
Result
Re-run after the colour work, and it was owed: the contrast audit of 18 Aug walked every text element on the page except text inside inline SVGs, which is 65 diagrams.

Structurally clean. All 65 carry role="img" and an aria-label. Only two presentation-attribute colours existed in the whole corpus.

Two real failures, both fixed. Ten <text> elements across six sections had a hardcoded white fill sitting on a var(--accent) rect — 3.19:1 in dark before this phase and 2.66:1 after, because COL-04 deliberately lightened the dark accent. That is exactly the defect --on-accent exists to solve, in the one place a stylesheet token could not reach. And two labels in report-suites/02 were white on a --success fill at 2.54:1 in both themes — --success is a cross-world constant and identical in light and dark, so nothing about the theme was ever going to rescue them. The fill moved to --success-ink and both labels to --on-accent: 6.25:1 light, 9.29:1 dark.
